Getting set up

From "I want to invest in India" to "I'm ready"

Investing in India as an NRI is very doable — once the groundwork is in place. This checklist walks you through the practical setup, in roughly the order it should happen. Tick each item as you complete it. It explains what to put in place, not what to buy.

It is written primarily for NRIs in the UAE, the GCC and the wider Middle East & MEA region. NRIs in the US, Canada and a few other jurisdictions face additional rules (such as FATCA-related restrictions) and should seek jurisdiction-specific guidance.

A Confirm Status & Documents

Everything else follows from your residency status and core documents.
Confirm your residential status (NRI / RNOR / Resident) for the relevant year — it drives accounts, taxation and eligibility.
Keep a valid passport, visa / residence permit and overseas address proof ready.
Ensure you have a valid PAN (Permanent Account Number) — essential for investing and tax in India.
Have recent photographs and your overseas bank/salary proof available for verification where required.

B Banking Foundations

The right NRI bank accounts are the gateway to everything else.
Open an NRE account for your foreign earnings — freely repatriable (principal and interest can be sent back abroad).
Open an NRO account for India-sourced income (rent, dividends, etc.), noting it has repatriation limits and different tax treatment.
Consider an FCNR (B) deposit if you wish to hold funds in foreign currency and avoid rupee conversion risk on that money.
Convert or re-designate any old resident savings accounts to NRO once your status changes — operating them as resident accounts is not permitted.

C Investment & Compliance Setup

The accounts and approvals that let you actually invest, compliantly.
Complete NRI KYC with your overseas address and documents (this differs from resident KYC).
Decide your investment route — for example, mutual funds (NRE/NRO basis) or direct equity via the PIS / approved route through your bank.
For direct equity, set up a demat & trading account linked to the appropriate NRI bank account.
Note that some products / schemes may not accept NRIs from certain jurisdictions — check eligibility before applying.
Explore whether the GIFT City / IFSC route suits you — a Gulf-friendly gateway for certain global and India-linked investments.

D Taxation & DTAA

Get the paperwork right to be taxed correctly — and avoid being taxed twice.
Understand how TDS (tax deducted at source) applies to NRIs — it often differs from resident rates.
If your country of residence has a DTAA with India, gather the documents to claim its benefits: a Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) and Form 10F.
Keep records of capital gains on your investments — classified short- or long-term, with different treatment.
Check whether you need to file an income-tax return in India for the year, and the due dates.

E Repatriation Readiness

Moving money out should be planned, not improvised.
Know the repatriation rules — NRE funds are freely repatriable; NRO has annual limits and a documentation process.
Keep Form 15CA / 15CB and supporting documents ready where required for outward remittances from NRO funds.
Maintain a clear source-of-funds trail for amounts you may want to send abroad later.

F Estate & Nomination

Protect your family across borders.
Add nominations to every India account, investment and policy.
Consider a Will covering your India assets, mindful of cross-border succession.
Keep a consolidated record of India holdings accessible to your family.
What next? Each item is a setup step, not a recommendation. Which investments (if any) suit you depends on your goals, time horizon and risk profile, and on a proper conversation with a qualified professional. Tax and FEMA rules change — verify the current position before acting.
