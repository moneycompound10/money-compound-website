How to use this

Audit your retirement in 27 questions

Most people discover gaps in their retirement plan far too late to fix them comfortably. This checklist helps you find those gaps early. Work through each point honestly and tick the box only if you can confidently say “yes, that’s handled.” Then count your ticks and read the score guide on the last page.

It is organised around the four pillars of a retirement plan: the income you will live on, the corpus that funds it, the healthcare that protects it, and the estate arrangements that pass it on. This is an awareness tool — it tells you where to look, not what to buy.

A Retirement Income

Will money keep coming in once your salary stops? These points test the income side of your plan.
1I have estimated my monthly expenses in retirement (in today’s terms), separating essentials from lifestyle spends.
2I have identified every income source I expect in retirement — pension, rent, interest, dividends, annuities, business income and so on.
3I have checked that my expected income will cover at least my essential expenses, not only the discretionary ones.
4I have planned for a long retirement — potentially 25–30 years or more — so my money does not run out too soon (longevity).
5I have thought about how I will draw an income from my savings once the salary stops (a withdrawal approach).
6I have factored in inflation — recognising that my cost of living will keep rising right through retirement.
7I have considered how a non-earning spouse or dependant would be supported if I were no longer around.

B Retirement Corpus

Is the pot of money behind that income big enough, and growing on schedule?
8I have a target corpus figure — an estimate of how much I need accumulated by my retirement date.
9I am tracking my progress — I know roughly whether my current savings and investments are on pace toward that target.
10I invest regularly and specifically for retirement, rather than relying only on whatever is left over.
11My retirement money is kept separate from short-term goals, so it does not get disturbed for other needs.
12I step up my contributions as my income grows, rather than leaving them fixed for years.
13I understand the risk profile of my retirement investments and am satisfied it suits my time horizon.
14I hold an emergency fund, so a sudden expense will not force me to break my retirement investments early.

C Healthcare

Medical costs are the most common reason retirement plans get derailed. These points stress-test your protection.
15I have adequate health insurance that will continue to cover me into and through retirement.
16I know whether my employer health cover ends at retirement, and I have a plan to replace it if it does.
17My cover amount reflects realistic future medical costs — which tend to rise faster than general inflation.
18I have considered cover for critical illness and long-term / elder-care needs, not just hospitalisation.
19I have set aside a medical buffer for out-of-pocket costs that insurance may not fully cover.
20My spouse and dependants are also adequately covered for health.

D Estate & Succession

Will what you have built reach the people you intend, smoothly and without dispute?
21I have a valid, up-to-date Will.
22I have recorded nominations on all bank accounts, investments, insurance policies and my demat account.
23My nominations and my Will are consistent with each other, with no conflicts.
24My family knows where my records, documents and access details are kept.
25I maintain a consolidated list of all my assets, liabilities, accounts and policies in one place.
26I have thought about how assets will pass to my heirs smoothly (joint holding, succession arrangements).
27I review the whole plan after major life events — marriage, children, a property purchase, or a change in residency / NRI status.
